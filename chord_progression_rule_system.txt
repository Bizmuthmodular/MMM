CHORD PROGRESSION RULE SYSTEM
For software that builds progressions in a given scale and genre.
===============================================================


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PART 1: SCALES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Each scale is defined by its interval pattern from the root.
W = whole step (2 semitones), H = half step (1 semitone), A = augmented 2nd (3 semitones).

1.1 MAJOR (Ionian)
  Pattern:    W W H W W W H
  Notes (C):  C D E F G A B
  Character:  Bright, stable, resolved.

1.2 NATURAL MINOR (Aeolian)
  Pattern:    W H W W H W W
  Notes (A):  A B C D E F G
  Character:  Dark, melancholic, familiar.

1.3 DORIAN
  Pattern:    W H W W W H W
  Notes (D):  D E F G A B C
  Character:  Minor with raised 6th. Bittersweet, modern minor.
  Relation:   Mode 2 of major scale.

1.4 MIXOLYDIAN
  Pattern:    W W H W W H W
  Notes (G):  G A B C D E F
  Character:  Major with flat 7th. Unresolved, bluesy.
  Relation:   Mode 5 of major scale.

1.5 LYDIAN
  Pattern:    W W W H W W H
  Notes (F):  F G A B C D E
  Character:  Major with raised 4th. Dreamy, floating.
  Relation:   Mode 4 of major scale.

1.6 HARMONIC MINOR
  Pattern:    W H W W H A H
  Notes (A):  A B C D E F G#
  Character:  Natural minor with raised 7th. Dramatic, classical.
  Special:    Produces a natural V7 dominant. Augmented 2nd interval (F→G#) gives exotic color.

1.7 MELODIC MINOR
  Pattern:    W H W W W W H
  Notes (A):  A B C D E F# G#
  Character:  Minor with raised 6th and 7th. Smooth, sophisticated.
  Special:    Produces imMaj7, m6, and Lydian Dominant chords natively.

1.8 LYDIAN DOMINANT
  Pattern:    W W W H W H W
  Notes (F):  F G A B C D Eb
  Character:  Mixolydian with raised 4th. Floaty and unresolved simultaneously.
  Relation:   Mode 4 of melodic minor.
  Special:    The scale of the tritone substitution dominant chord.

1.9 DIMINISHED (Octatonic, half-whole)
  Pattern:    H W H W H W H W  (8 notes)
  Notes (C):  C Db Eb E F# G A Bb
  Character:  Maximally tense, symmetrical, ambiguous tonic.
  Special:    Every note is equidistant; chord can resolve to multiple roots.
              Used over dominant 7th chords for extreme tension.

1.10 LOCRIAN
  Pattern:    H W W H W W W
  Notes (B):  B C D E F G A
  Character:  Unstable, unresolved, no strong tonic.
  Special:    Only useful as source of vii° chord or for Horror applications.
              Not recommended as a home key except in Horror genre.

1.11 DOUBLE HARMONIC (Byzantine / Arabic)
  Pattern:    H A H W H A H
  Notes (C):  C Db E F G Ab B
  Character:  Two augmented 2nd intervals. Deeply exotic, tense, unsettling.
  Special:    Produces tritone relationships and 6-semitone intervals.
              Primary scale for Horror genre.

1.12 HUNGARIAN MINOR (Double Harmonic Minor)
  Pattern:    W H A H H A H
  Notes (A):  A B C D# E F G#
  Character:  Harmonic minor with raised 4th. Dark, mysterious, Eastern European.
  Special:    Contains tritone (A→D#). Strong Horror and dramatic tension applications.

1.13 TRITONE SCALE (Prometheus / Scriabin)
  Pattern:    W W W H W H  (hexatonic, 6 notes)
  Notes (C):  C D E F# A Bb
  Character:  Built around the tritone interval. Ambiguous, unstable, cinematic.
  Special:    No strong tonic. Tritone (C→F#) is the defining interval.
              Useful in Horror for sustained unease rather than shock.

PART 2: CHORD CONSTRUCTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

2.1 THE ADD-SKIP RULE
  Build chords by stacking every other note of the scale from the root:
    3-note (triad):   root + 3rd + 5th
    4-note (7th):     root + 3rd + 5th + 7th
    4-note (no5):     root + 3rd + 7th       [drop 5th — standard jazz voicing]
    4-note (6th):     root + 3rd + 5th + 6th [no 7th]
    5-note (9th):     root + 3rd + 5th + 7th + 9th
    4-note (9th/no5): root + 3rd + 7th + 9th [drop 5th — preferred 4-note 9th voicing]
    5-note (6/9):     root + 3rd + 5th + 6th + 9th [no 7th]
    4-note (6/9/no5): root + 3rd + 6th + 9th [drop 5th]

2.2 CHORD TYPES BY INTERVAL STACK
  Major triad:        M3 + m3         (4 + 3 semitones)
  Minor triad:        m3 + M3         (3 + 4 semitones)
  Diminished triad:   m3 + m3         (3 + 3 semitones)
  Augmented triad:    M3 + M3         (4 + 4 semitones)
  Major 7th (maj7):   M3 + m3 + M3
  Minor 7th (m7):     m3 + M3 + m3
  Dominant 7th (7):   M3 + m3 + m3    [the most tension-loaded standard chord]
  Minor-Major 7th (mMaj7): m3 + M3 + M3  [eerie, from melodic/harmonic minor]
  Half-diminished (m7b5): m3 + m3 + M3
  Fully diminished (dim7): m3 + m3 + m3  [all equal, symmetrical]
  Augmented 7th (aug7): M3 + M3 + m2  [from whole tone / exotic scales]

2.3 THE 5TH IS OPTIONAL
  The 5th of any chord can be dropped in 4-note voicings without changing
  the chord's harmonic identity or function. This is standard practice.
  Rule: if chord note count must be exactly 4, drop the 5th first.

PART 3: DIATONIC CHORD SETS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Chords listed as Roman numerals with 7th quality. Triad quality in brackets.

3.1 MAJOR (in C)
  I    Cmaj7   [major]
  ii   Dm7     [minor]
  iii  Em7     [minor]
  IV   Fmaj7   [major]
  V    G7      [major]       ← dominant 7th
  vi   Am7     [minor]
  vii° Bm7b5   [diminished]  ← half-diminished

3.2 NATURAL MINOR (in A)
  i    Am7     [minor]
  ii°  Bm7b5   [diminished]
  III  Cmaj7   [major]
  iv   Dm7     [minor]
  v    Em7     [minor]       ← no dominant function
  VI   Fmaj7   [major]
  VII  G7      [major]
  V7*  E7      [major]       ← added chord: raise 3rd of v to get dominant (same as harmonic minor trick)

3.3 DORIAN (in D)
  i    Dm7     [minor]
  ii   Em7     [minor]
  III  Fmaj7   [major]
  IV   G7      [major]       ← dominant color on IV is characteristic of Dorian
  v    Am7     [minor]
  vi°  Bm7b5   [diminished]
  VII  Cmaj7   [major]

3.4 MIXOLYDIAN (in G)
  I    G7      [major]       ← tonic is itself a dominant 7th — defining feature
  ii   Am7     [minor]
  iii° Bm7b5   [diminished]
  IV   Cmaj7   [major]
  v    Dm7     [minor]
  vi   Em7     [minor]
  VII  Fmaj7   [major]

3.5 LYDIAN (in F)
  I    Fmaj7#11 [major]      ← raised 4th gives the #11 extension
  II   G7       [major]      ← major chord on II is defining feature of Lydian
  iii  Am7      [minor]
  iv°  Bm7b5    [diminished]
  V    Cmaj7    [major]      ← note: no dominant V7 in Lydian
  vi   Dm7      [minor]
  vii  Em7      [minor]

3.6 HARMONIC MINOR (in A)
  i    AmMaj7  [minor]       ← minor-major 7th, eerie quality
  ii°  Bm7b5   [diminished]
  III  Cmaj7#5 [augmented]
  iv   Dm7     [minor]
  V    E7      [major]       ← natural dominant 7th
  VI   Fmaj7   [major]
  vii° G#dim7  [fully dim]   ← fully diminished 7th

3.7 MELODIC MINOR (in A)
  i    AmMaj7  [minor]
  ii   Bm7     [minor]
  III  Cmaj7#5 [augmented]
  IV   D7      [major]       ← Lydian dominant chord
  V    E7      [major]
  vi°  F#m7b5  [diminished]
  vii° G#m7b5  [diminished]

3.8 LYDIAN DOMINANT (in F, mode 4 of C melodic minor)
  I    F7#11   [dominant]    ← defining chord of this scale
  II   G7      [dominant]
  iii° Am7b5   [diminished]
  iv°  Bm7b5   [diminished]
  V    Cmaj7   [major]
  vi   Dm7     [minor]
  vii  Em7     [minor]

3.9 DIMINISHED (in C, half-whole)
  Chords repeat every 3 semitones due to symmetry.
  Primary chords: C7, Eb7, F#7, A7  (all dominant 7th, all interchangeable)
  All can be treated as dominant functioning chords resolving to any of 4 roots.
  Also produces: Cdim7, Ebdim7, F#dim7, Adim7

3.10 DOUBLE HARMONIC (in C)
  I    Cmaj7   [major]
  bII  DbMaj7#5 [augmented]  ← flat 2nd, very tense
  III  E7      [dominant]
  IV   Fm7     [minor]
  V    G7      [dominant]
  bVI  AbMaj7  [major]
  VII  Bdim7   [fully dim]
  Note: tritone C→F# and augmented 2nds give this scale its Horror character.

3.11 HUNGARIAN MINOR (in A)
  i    AmMaj7  [minor]
  ii°  Bm7b5   [diminished]
  III  CMaj7#5 [augmented]
  #iv° D#dim7  [fully dim]   ← raised 4th, tritone from root
  V    E7      [dominant]
  VI   FMaj7   [major]
  vii° G#dim7  [fully dim]

PART 4: FUNCTIONAL HARMONY SYSTEM
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

4.1 THE THREE FAMILIES
  TONIC (T):       Stable, resolved. Strong chords. Home base.
  SUBDOMINANT (SD): Mild tension. Weak chords. Move away from home.
  DOMINANT (D):    Strong tension. Weakest chords. Want to resolve to T.

4.2 FUNCTION ASSIGNMENTS BY SCALE DEGREE

  MAJOR KEY:
    T:  I, iii, vi
    SD: ii, IV, vii°
    D:  V, vii°          (vii° can be SD or D depending on context)

  NATURAL MINOR KEY:
    T:  i, III
    SD: ii°, iv, VI, VII, v
    D:  V7 (added chord only — the raised dominant)

  DORIAN:
    T:  i, III
    SD: ii, IV, VI
    D:  V (weak dominant — Dorian has no natural leading tone)
    Note: IV is a dominant 7th chord in Dorian but functions as SD here.

  MIXOLYDIAN:
    T:  I, vi
    SD: ii°, IV, v
    D:  No strong dominant (I itself is a dom7). VII acts as a weak D substitute.
    Note: Progressions in Mixolydian often avoid strong resolution intentionally.

  LYDIAN:
    T:  I, V, iii
    SD: II, vi, vii
    D:  No dominant V7 — II7 (Lydian dominant) is the closest substitute.

  HARMONIC MINOR:
    T:  i, III
    SD: ii°, iv, VI
    D:  V7, vii° (fully diminished)

  MELODIC MINOR:
    T:  i, III
    SD: ii, IV, vi°
    D:  V7, vii°

  LYDIAN DOMINANT:
    T:  I (inherently unresolved — this scale lives in D function)
    SD: II, vi
    D:  I itself, iii°, iv°
    Note: Lydian Dominant is most useful as a single-chord color or passing scale,
          not as a home key for full progressions.

  DIMINISHED:
    All chords are D function. No stable tonic.
    Use only for tension passages, transitions, or Horror.

  DOUBLE HARMONIC / HUNGARIAN MINOR / TRITONE:
    T:  I
    SD: IV, bVI (Double Harmonic), VI (Hungarian)
    D:  V7, bII (Double Harmonic — tritone sub), vii°, #iv° (Hungarian)
    Note: bII as D function is the "Neapolitan" move — very effective in Horror.

4.3 STRONG-WEAK RULE
  Strong chords = Tonic family.
  Weak chords = Subdominant + Dominant family.
  Pattern: Strong → Weak → Weak → ... → Strong
  Any sequence alternating strong and weak is harmonically valid.

4.4 T–SD–D–T RULE
  The most directional pattern.
  Start on T, move to SD, move to D, resolve to T.
  Can be extended: T → SD → D → T → SD → D → T ...
  The cadence (D→T at the end) is the strongest moment of resolution.

4.5 CADENCE TYPES (ending formulas)
  Authentic:    V(7) → I     [strongest resolution]
  Plagal:       IV → I       [softer, "amen" cadence]
  Half:         any → V      [ends on tension, unresolved]
  Deceptive:    V → vi       [surprise — resolves to relative minor instead]
  Tritone sub:  bII7 → I     [jazz substitute for authentic cadence]
  Phrygian:     bII → i      [used in Horror and flamenco]

4.6 DOMINANT RULE
  The V7 chord is the most tension-loaded standard chord.
  In keys without a natural V7 (natural minor, Dorian, Mixolydian, Lydian):
    → Raise the 3rd of the v chord by one semitone to create V7.
    → This is always valid as a borrowed dominant.
  In Horror scales, bII7 (Neapolitan dominant) can substitute or combine with V7.


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PART 5: GENRE PROFILES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

For each genre: preferred scales, preferred chord types, preferred patterns,
cadence tendencies, and special rules.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5.1 POP
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Scales:       Major (primary), Natural Minor, Dorian, Mixolydian, Lydian (occasional)
Chord types:  Triads and 7th chords. 9th chords for color. Avoid exotic extensions.
Pattern:      Strong-Weak strongly preferred. T–SD–D–T for emotional climax.
Cadences:     Authentic (V→I) and Plagal (IV→I) most common. Deceptive for surprise.
Length:       4-bar progressions most common. 8-bar for verse/chorus.
Special rules:
  - Start on I or vi (never on V or vii°).
  - IV→I (plagal) is very idiomatic in pop choruses.
  - Borrowed chords from parallel minor are common (e.g. bVII in major key).
  - Avoid fully diminished chords and half-diminished in lead positions.
  - 6/9 chords acceptable on I for a modern pop feel.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5.2 NEO-SOUL
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Scales:       Dorian (primary), Major, Natural Minor, Melodic Minor, Mixolydian,
              Lydian (color), Harmonic Minor (borrowed chords)
Chord types:  9th chords (no5) preferred. maj7, m7, mMaj7, m6, 6/9 all standard.
              Triads are too plain — always extend to at least 7th.
Pattern:      T–SD–D–T for structured progressions.
              Strong-Weak for vamps and loops (2–4 chords cycling).
Cadences:     Deceptive cadences common. Tritone subs welcome.
              Authentic cadence less common — resolution is often avoided or softened.
Length:       2–4 chord vamps are idiomatic. 8-bar for song sections.
Special rules:
  - The ii–V–I (Dm7→G7→Cmaj7) and its extensions are central.
  - Minor ii–V–i (Dm7b5→G7→Cm) equally important.
  - m6 and mMaj7 chords signal sophistication — use freely on i chords.
  - 6/9 on I or i gives the characteristic neo-soul openness.
  - Chord substitutions (tritone, relative, parallel) are expected, not exceptional.
  - Voicing matters: drop-2, spread voicings preferred over close position.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5.3 JAZZ
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Scales:       All scales valid. Melodic Minor and its modes are essential.
              Lydian Dominant, Diminished, Harmonic Minor used constantly.
Chord types:  7th minimum. 9th, 11th, 13th standard. Altered chords (7#9, 7b9,
              7#11, 7b13) over dominants. All chord types from all scales valid.
Pattern:      ii–V–I is the fundamental unit (can replace T–SD–D–T).
              Minor ii–V–i equally important.
              Chain of ii–V's moving around the circle of 5ths is idiomatic.
Cadences:     Tritone substitution standard (bII7→I replaces V7→I).
              Deceptive, backdoor (bVII7→I), and Plagal all used.
Length:       8-bar and 16-bar forms standard (based on song form: AABA, blues, etc.)
Special rules:
  - The dominant V7 chord can be altered: add b9, #9, b13, #11 freely.
  - Diminished 7th chords resolve by moving any voice up a half step.
  - Tritone substitution: replace any V7 with the dom7 chord a tritone away.
  - Chord quality can be changed on any diatonic chord for color (modal interchange).
  - Turnarounds (I→vi→ii→V or variations) are idiomatic at phrase endings.
  - Reharmonization is expected — the same melody can support many chord choices.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5.4 TRANCE
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Scales:       Natural Minor (primary), Major, Dorian, Harmonic Minor,
              Mixolydian (euphoric sections)
Chord types:  Triads common (power and open voicings for synth pads).
              7th chords for color. Avoid complex extensions — clarity preferred.
Pattern:      Strong-Weak strongly preferred for looping.
              T–SD–D–T for builds and drops.
Cadences:     Authentic (V→I or V→i) for drops.
              Half cadence (ending on V) for sustained tension before drop.
Length:       4-bar and 8-bar loops. Progressions repeat many times.
Special rules:
  - Progressions must work as loops — last chord must connect back to first.
  - Minor i–VI–III–VII and i–VII–VI–VII are archetypal trance patterns.
  - Harmonic minor's V7 is preferred over natural minor's v for drops.
  - Pedal point (sustained root bass note under changing chords) is idiomatic.
  - Avoid half-diminished and fully diminished in main progressions —
    use only for brief tension moments in transitions.
  - In major/euphoric sections: I–V–vi–IV is the defining pop-trance loop.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5.5 HORROR
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Scales:       Double Harmonic (primary), Hungarian Minor, Harmonic Minor,
              Diminished, Locrian, Tritone Scale
Chord types:  Fully diminished 7th (dim7) — primary tension chord.
              Half-diminished (m7b5), augmented triads, mMaj7.
              Triads acceptable. Avoid maj7 and 9th chords (too warm/resolved).
Pattern:      T–SD–D–T less important — ambiguity and avoidance of resolution preferred.
              Chromatic voice leading (moving voices by half steps) over harmonic function.
              Parallel motion (all voices move in same direction) for dread.
Cadences:     Avoid authentic cadences — resolution breaks tension.
              Phrygian cadence (bII→i) is primary.
              Deceptive cadences useful for shock.
              Half cadence (leave on V or vii°) for sustained unease.
Length:       Variable — short 2-bar cells that repeat and mutate, or long slow movements.
Special rules:
  - The tritone interval (6 semitones) is the primary source of unease.
    Build chords and progressions that emphasize tritone relationships.
  - bII (Neapolitan chord) is extremely effective — a major chord a half step
    above the tonic. Use bII→i as the primary cadential move.
  - Fully diminished 7th chords are symmetrical — exploit this by resolving
    them to unexpected roots (any of 4 possible resolutions).
  - Augmented chords create unease through the absence of a stable 5th.
  - Chromatic mediant relationships (chords whose roots are a 3rd apart with
    no common key) create sudden tonal shifts — very effective for horror.
  - Do not feel obligated to establish a tonic — floating tonality is valid.
  - Cluster chords (semitone-adjacent notes) acceptable for extreme tension.
  - The interval of a minor 2nd (half step) in a chord voicing creates maximum dissonance.

PART 6: PROGRESSION CONSTRUCTION ALGORITHM
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

INPUT:  scale, root, genre, length (number of chords)
OUTPUT: sequence of chords with function labels

STEP 1 — BUILD DIATONIC CHORD SET
  Apply add-skip rule to the chosen scale from the chosen root.
  Generate 4-note chords (drop 5th if needed for voice count).
  Label each chord with its type and scale degree.

STEP 2 — ASSIGN FUNCTIONS
  Apply function table from Part 4.2 for the chosen scale.
  Tag each chord as T, SD, or D.

STEP 3 — SELECT PATTERN
  Based on genre (Part 5):
    Pop / Trance:    use Strong-Weak
    Neo-soul / Jazz: use T–SD–D–T or ii–V–I chains
    Horror:          use chromatic/Phrygian logic, avoid clean T–SD–D–T

STEP 4 — PLACE FIRST CHORD
  Always start on a T chord (I or i) except:
    Horror: may start on any chord including D or ambiguous.
    Jazz: may start on ii for an anacrusis into ii–V–I.

STEP 5 — FILL MIDDLE CHORDS
  Follow the chosen pattern.
  Any chord within the correct functional family is valid at each position.
  Prefer stepwise root motion (up or down a 2nd or 3rd) over leaps where possible.
  Leaps of a 4th or 5th are also strong (circle of 5ths motion).

STEP 6 — PLACE CADENCE
  Apply cadence appropriate to genre (Part 4.5).
  Last two chords should be D→T (authentic) or genre-appropriate substitute.
  Exception: Trance loops — last chord must connect smoothly back to first.

STEP 7 — VALIDATE LOOP (Trance only)
  Check that final chord connects back to first chord via a valid T or SD function.
  If not, adjust final chord or insert a bridge chord.

STEP 8 — APPLY CHORD EXTENSIONS
  Based on genre chord type preference (Part 5):
    Pop:     upgrade triads to 7th chords where appropriate
    Neo-soul: upgrade all chords to 7th minimum, prefer 9th and 6/9
    Jazz:    apply extensions and alterations, especially on V chords
    Trance:  keep triads or simple 7ths
    Horror:  prefer dim7, m7b5, augmented; avoid maj7 and 9th


═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
PART 7: SPECIAL TECHNIQUES (cross-genre)
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

7.1 MODAL INTERCHANGE (Borrowed Chords)
  Borrow a chord from the parallel scale (same root, different mode).
  Most common: borrow from parallel minor into major key.
  Examples in C major: Fm (from C minor), Ab (from C minor), bVII (Bb).
  Rule: borrowed chord retains its function from its source scale.

7.2 TRITONE SUBSTITUTION
  Replace any dominant V7 chord with the dom7 chord a tritone (6 semitones) away.
  Example: G7 → Db7 (both resolve to C).
  The two chords share the same tritone interval (different enharmonic spelling).
  Applicable in: Jazz (standard), Neo-soul (common), Pop (occasional), Horror (natural).

7.3 SECONDARY DOMINANTS
  Apply a V7 chord to any diatonic chord, not just I.
  Example: to approach Dm7 (ii in C), use A7 (V7 of ii) first.
  Formula: find V7 of target chord, insert before target.
  Applicable in: Jazz (essential), Neo-soul (common), Pop (occasional).

7.4 CHROMATIC MEDIANT
  Move between chords whose roots are a major or minor 3rd apart
  and share no key relationship.
  Example: C major → Ab major (roots a minor 3rd apart, no common key).
  Creates sudden, colorful, non-functional shifts.
  Applicable in: Horror (primary technique), Neo-soul (color), Jazz (reharmonization).

7.5 PEDAL POINT
  Sustain the root note in the bass while chords change above it.
  Creates tension as non-tonic chords sound over the pedal.
  Release pedal on resolution for maximum effect.
  Applicable in: Trance (idiomatic), Horror (dread), Jazz (modal sections).

7.6 NEAPOLITAN CHORD (bII)
  A major chord built on the flat 2nd degree of the scale.
  Example in A minor: Bb major.
  Traditionally moves to V7 then i (bII→V7→i).
  In Horror, bII→i directly (Phrygian cadence) is primary.
  Applicable in: Horror (essential), Jazz (reharmonization), Neo-soul (color).
